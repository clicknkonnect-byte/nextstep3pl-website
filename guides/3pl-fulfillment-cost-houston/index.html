How Much Does 3PL Fulfillment Cost in Houston? (2026 Guide)

Storage, receiving, pick-and-pack, add-ons and shipping — what each piece of a Houston 3PL bill really costs in 2026, and how to estimate yours.

Published 2026-08-016 min read

"How much does it cost?" is the first question every brand asks a 3PL, and the honest answer is: it depends on how your product moves. But that doesn't mean you can't get a clear picture. Third-party logistics pricing is built from a small set of predictable line items, and once you understand them you can estimate your Houston fulfillment cost within a reasonable range before you ever request a quote.

Here's how the pieces fit together in 2026, using current US 3PL market averages.

The core line items

Nearly every 3PL bill is built from four buckets. A trustworthy 3PL itemizes each one instead of hiding them in a single blended rate:

  • Storage — charged per pallet, bin or shelf, per month. Standard pallet storage runs around $18/pallet/month; climate-managed space is higher (around $32); small-item bin and shelf storage is lower (around $11/unit).
  • Receiving — a one-time fee to unload, count and shelve inbound inventory, often around $35 per pallet.
  • Pick & pack (fulfillment) — the per-order fee to pull items and pack the box. A common structure is a set fee for the first item (around $2.50) plus a small charge for each additional item (around $0.65).
  • Shipping postage — the carrier cost to actually move the parcel, billed at the 3PL's discounted multi-carrier rates and passed through.

The add-ons that change the number

Beyond the core, a few extras show up depending on your product and brand:

  • Branded packaging or inserts (often around $1.25 per order)
  • Kitting or gift-set assembly (around $2.50 per build)
  • Amazon FBA prep — labeling, poly-bagging and carton prep to spec
  • Returns processing — receiving, inspection and restock

What actually drives your bill

Two brands shipping the same number of orders can have very different bills. The biggest levers are order volume, the average number of items per order (more items = more pick fees), how much and what type of storage you use, product size and weight (which drive postage), and where your customers are. This last one is where Houston helps: shipping from the center of the country puts most US customers in a lower shipping zone, which lowers postage — often the largest single line on the bill.

A quick worked example

Say you ship 1,500 orders a month, averaging 2 items each, with standard packaging, and you store 40 pallets. Your pick-and-pack runs about $2.50 + $0.65 = $3.15 per order, or roughly $4,725/month. Storage is about 40 × $18 = $720/month. Add one-time receiving when inventory lands, and pass-through postage on top. The exact figure depends on your SKUs, but the structure is that predictable — which is why an itemized quote beats a vague blended rate every time.
